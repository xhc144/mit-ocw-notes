What we have learned here:
(1.) We can find the normal modes by solving the eigenvalue problem for the symmetry matrix, S,
instead of M −1 k (given that SM −1 k = M −1 kS or [S, M −1 k] = 0)
(2.) This is a remarkable result: All two component system satisfy SM −1 k = M −1 kS and therefore
they all have the same eigenvectors!!! Once you’ve solved one, you have solved them all.

This equation is known as a “dispersion relation” because it relates the wave number k and the
frequency ω. Let’s study this equation further:
(1) There are an infinite number of normal modes: Each k gives a normal mode
(2) Amplitude: Aj = 2i 1
                         (eijka − e−ijka ) = sin jka (to make Aj real)




Since both Aj = β j = eijka and A−j are eigenvectors of M −1 k with eigenvalue ω 2 = 2ω02 (1 −
cos ka) ⇒ linear combinations of them are also eigenvectors of M −1 k! (see Georgi pg. 113)
(3) Maximum frequency: ω 2 = 4ω02 → which occurs when cos ka = −1
(4) cos ka = 1 or β = 1 gives the minimum frequency: ω 2 = 0. Physically this means all masses
are moving in the same direction.


All possible motions: linear combinations of all normal modes. Each normal mode: standing waves.
