Problem 10.4 (20 pts)
A small amount of oil (n = 1.45) drops on the smooth surface of a lake (n = 1.33). It forms a ﬁlm of
continously dminishing thickness as it spreads out across the surface. An observer watches the changing
appearance of the skylight (white) reﬂected at near normal incidence. See the wavelengths in Table 1.
 a. Initially, when the ﬁlm is thick, the reﬂected light appears white to the naked eye. When viewed
    through a narrow band ﬁlter centered on red, the reﬂected intensity changes with time. Through the
    ﬁlter the reﬂection is observed to start out bright, go through 10 minima, and return to maximum.
    How much has the thickness of the ﬁlm changed during this time?
 b. Only when the ﬁlm has thinned suﬃciently does it appear colored to the unaided eye. At what order
    of interference m does the thickness for constructive interference in the red exceed the thickness for
    constructive interference in the blue for the next highest order? (For thickness greater than this,
    diﬀerent portions of the visible spectrum overlap in the reﬂected light.)
 c. Just when the ﬁlm on the ﬂat surface appears green, a wind generated surface wave crosses the ﬁeld
    of view. As a result the color oscillates with time. Toward which end of spectrum does the color
    change? (Recall that for thin ﬁlm intereference, the optical path length diﬀerence is proportional to
    the cosine of the angle the ray makes with normal in the ﬁlm.)
