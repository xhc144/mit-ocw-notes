   1. Let f : R → R. Prove that the collection of sets

             A = E ⊂ R | f −1(E) is Lebesgue measurable

       is a σ-algebra.

   2. Let E ⊂ R, and assume that m∗(E) < ∞. Prove that E is measurable if and only if
      for every ϵ > 0 there exists a ﬁnite union of open intervals U such that m ∗(UΔE) < ϵ.
     This result is known as Littlewood’s ﬁrst principle: every measurable set is nearly a
      ﬁnite union of open intervals.
     Hint: To prove the converse direction, let A ⊂ R, and prove that for every ϵ > 0,

              m∗ (A ∩ E) + m∗ (A ∩ Ec) ≤ m∗ (A) + ϵ.


    You may use without proof the fact that a ﬁnite union of open intervals is measurable.
     This is covered in Lecture 8 which has been moved to Week 5.

   3. Let E be a measurable set.

       (a) Prove that for all x ∈ R, E + x is measurable.

      (b) Prove that for all r > 0, rE := {ry | y ∈ E} is measurable.
