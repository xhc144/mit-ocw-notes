For finite sets \(A\) and \(B\),  \(\;A \text{ inj } B\) is equivalent to:
  

 
 Exercise 1 
   &nbsp;   There exists an injective relation from \(A \) to \(B \)
      &nbsp;  

   &nbsp;   There exists a total injective relation from \(A \) to \( B\)
      &nbsp;  

   &nbsp;   There exists an injective function from \(A \) to \(B \)
       &nbsp;  

   &nbsp;   There exists a surjective relation from \(B \) to \(A \)
      &nbsp;  

   &nbsp;   There exists a total surjective relation from \(B \) to \(A \)      
        &nbsp;  

   &nbsp;   There exists a surjective function from \(B \) to \(A \)
       &nbsp;  

   &nbsp;   There exists a bijection from \(A \) to some subset of \(B \)  
        &nbsp;  

   &nbsp;   There exists a bijection from \(B \) to some subset of \(A \)
       &nbsp;  

   &nbsp;   \(\mid A \mid\, \le\, \mid B \mid \)
       &nbsp;  

   &nbsp;   \(\mid A \mid\, >\, \mid B \mid \)
        &nbsp;  

   &nbsp;   \(\mid A \mid\, =\, \mid B \mid \)
       &nbsp;  

 

 


 An injective function from \(A \) to \(B \) or a surjective relation from \(B \) to \(A \) do not guarantee \(A \text{ inj } B\).
