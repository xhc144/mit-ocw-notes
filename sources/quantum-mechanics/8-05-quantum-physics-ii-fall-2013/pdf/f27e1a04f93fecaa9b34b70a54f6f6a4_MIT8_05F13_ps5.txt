distinguish the two cases let 
Real case: (u, Su) = 0 , for all u , 
Complex case: (v, T v) = 0 , for all v 
We ﬁrst want to examine the case of dimension two to see in a simple example why the 
theorem is true and why it fails for real vector space. So we will consider two-by-two 
matrices and two-component vectors. 
(a) Let S be represented by a real matrix Sij and u by the two real components ui 
with i, j = 1, 2. For the complex case let T be represented by a complex coeﬃcient 
matrix Tij and v by the two complex components vi with i, j = 1, 2.
