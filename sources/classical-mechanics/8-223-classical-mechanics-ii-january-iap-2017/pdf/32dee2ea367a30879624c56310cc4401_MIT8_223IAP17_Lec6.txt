Last time we talked about several instances in which a symmetry of space or 
time resulted in a conserved quantity; conservation of energy, momentum, and 
angular momentum come from homogeneous time and space, and isotropic space. 
Each of these is an example of 
Noether’s theorem which states that “every differentiable 
symmetry of the action of a physical system has a corre­
sponding conservation law.” 
Today we will go through an example in which we use the things we talked 
about last time to ﬁnd the equations of motion of a diatomic molecule.

What this means for astronomy is that if you can measure the velocities of stars in 
a galaxy (actually, the velocity dispersion), you can measure T . From that you can 
compute U. You can then compare that to the potential you would expect from the 
stars you see. And you would discover that they don’t match at all! Dark matter! 
(factor ≈ 4). Simply put, stars in galaxies move too fast for the gravity of the stars. 
If there were nothing else, the galaxy would ﬂy apart!
