Problem 1: (10 points)
The smallest integer that cannot be exactly represented is n = β t + 1 (for base-β with a t-digit
mantissa). You might be tempted to think that β t cannot be represented, since a t-digit number,
at ﬁrst glance, only goes up to β t − 1 (e.g. three base-10 digits can only represent up to 999, not
1000). However, β t can be represented by β t−1 · β 1 , where the β 1 is absorbed in the exponent.

In IEEE single and double precision, β = 2 and t = 24 and 53, respectively, giving 224 + 1 =
16,777,217 and 253 + 1 = 9,007,199,254,740,993.

Evidence that n = 253 + 1 is not exactly represented but that numbers less than that are can
be presented in a variety of ways. In the pset1-solutions notebook, we check exactness by compar-
ing to Julia’s Int64 (built-in integer) type, which exactly represents values up to 263 − 1.
