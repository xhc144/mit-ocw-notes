How is "all rights reserved" content different from the rest of OCW content?

June 23, 2025 17:02 Updated

The vast majority of MIT OCW materials (unless marked to the contrary) are published under a Creative Commons license (CC BY-NC-SA). You are free to use, copy, and redistribute these unmarked materials in original or modified form ("derivative works") in whatever way you wish so long as you give credit to MIT and to the author (attribution or "BY"), you redistribute with the same Creative Commons license (share-alike or "SA"), and your use is non-commercial ("NC"). See our terms of use for complete information.

Some materials on OCW come from third-party sources and are covered by our Creative Commons license. This content is marked with a credit line "Courtesy of (copyright owner). Used with permission." If you modify or redistribute such material, you must carry forward this special attribution along with meeting the other requirements noted above.

On the other hand, "All rights reserved" content is different. This content is owned by someone else but is NOT covered by our Creative Commons license. Instead, we include it in OCW relying on U.S. copyright law's "fair use" doctrine. You can view it for your use, but unlike other OCW content, you do NOT automatically have the right to download, copy, modify, reuse, remix, or redistribute it.

If you'd like to learn more about how fair use has been applied to OCW content, please see the article in FAQs titled "What is the Code of Best Practices in Fair Use for OpenCourseWare?"
