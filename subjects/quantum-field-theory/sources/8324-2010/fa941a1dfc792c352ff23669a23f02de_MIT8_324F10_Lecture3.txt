We begin with some comments concerning gauge-symmetric theories:
1.          A U (1) local symmetry leads to the ﬁeld Aµ (x), mediating interactions between the charge ﬁelds ψi (x).
2.          No mass term is allowed for Aµ or the gauge symmetry is broken.
3.          It is possible to construct theories using other gauge invariant terms, for example
                                                                             ¯ µν γ µ γ ν ψ.
                                              ϵµνλρ Fµν Fλρ , (Fµν F µν )2 , ψF                                       (1)
            All of these terms can be written as a total derivative, or are non-renormalizable.
4.          Some of the terminology and constructs associated with gauge theories includes:
                                                  U (y, x) : Parallel transport,
                                                   Aµ (x) : Connection,
                                                  Fµν (x) : Curvature,
                                                   Dµ (x) : Covariant derivative.
            These objects form the mathematical framework of the ﬁbre bundle.
5.          Gauge symmetry is not really a symmetry. The phase of ψ (and part of Aµ ) does not carry physical
            information; there is a redundancy in our variables.
